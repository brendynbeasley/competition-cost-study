# 14 · Moore (1905) — The Personality of Antoine Augustin Cournot
*Quarterly Journal of Economics, 19(3), pp. 370–399* · Group: B Cournot revival & math of monopoly · Tier: 3

**Hook (memorize this line):** Moore's Cournot: half-blind, under-recognised, fiercely independent, winning his "case of 1838" late and losing that of 1863.

**Responds to:** Cournot's unpublished *Souvenirs* (written 1859), the main source; the long neglect of the *Recherches* (Walras: no French review as late as 1873); the revival marked by Fisher #11  
**Answered by:** Moore's own #15 (1906), which uses Cournot as the test case for competition theory

## Main claim
This is a biography built mainly from the *Souvenirs*. Moore presents Cournot as a model scholar who worked under discouraging conditions: no independent income for many years, and an eye infirmity that prevented sustained study. Few contemporaries recognised him, Poisson being the chief exception. His reputation lagged because of "masterful independence and absolute truthfulness" and his dislike of self-advertisement (p. 399).

## Argument in steps
1. **Model scholar (pp. 370–371).** Moore contrasts him with Darwin, who had leisure and income. Cournot's range ran from pure mathematics to probability, philosophy and economics.
2. **The three economic works (pp. 372–374).**
   - *Recherches*, 1838, printed while Cournot was at Grenoble. As late as 1873 no French critic had reviewed it (Walras).
   - *Principes de la théorie des richesses*, 1863, a version without symbols. It was poorly reviewed and even blamed for not citing others.
   - *Revue sommaire*, 1877.
   Boccardo, Jevons and Walras praised the *Recherches*.
3. **Career (pp. 375–391).**
   - Born at Gray, 28 August 1801. Entered the École Normale in 1821.
   - Spent ten years (1823–33) with Marshal Gouvion St Cyr.
   - With Poisson's patronage: the Lyons chair, then rector at Grenoble at 34, then inspector-general in 1838, then rector at Dijon in 1854.
   - Retired in 1862.
4. **Person (pp. 392–399).** Methodical habits; a snuff-box; a "fundamentally social" interest in science. Politically he moved over time (voted for Cavaignac in 1848); in religion he stayed conservative. He died in Paris on 30 March 1877.

## Key terms & distinctions
- ***Souvenirs***: Cournot's memoir (1859), Moore's main source.
- ***Recherches*** (1838) vs ***Principes*** (1863): the mathematical version and the later literary restatement.
- **Don't confuse:** this paper is biography, not Moore's analytical critique, which is #15. Also keep apart the *Recherches* date (1838) and Cournot's death (1877).
