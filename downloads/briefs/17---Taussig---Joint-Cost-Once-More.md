## Main claim
Taussig concedes that transport over *different stretches* (A–B and B–C) is not joint. So local and distance discrimination is a separate problem. But when different goods move over *one* stretch, most of the cost is joint. Those services are not "homogeneous" in the sense that matters, because their **demand schedules** differ. Railways are a "much restricted monopoly" that do not earn large monopoly revenue. So joint cost, not monopoly, explains most of the so-called discriminations.

## Argument in steps
1. **Two questions.** Is the supply in fact joint? And are the services homogeneous? (p. 378)
2. **Partial agreement.** A–B and B–C are not joint when each is a full operating division. Joint cost explains *classification*; it does not explain rates that ignore distance or run against it. Through traffic (A to B on the way to C) is a middle case, the long-and-short-haul problem. Taussig is not convinced that Pigou has refuted Hadley on it (p. 379).
3. **One stretch.** Over one stretch the cost is joint. Pigou already admits back loading as joint, and even there no service is "necessarily" produced with another. What makes supply joint is that most expenses are joint and a railway must offer every kind of service to make its investment pay. A coal-only or logging road would show no joint cost (p. 380).
4. **Homogeneity.** Cuts of beef, cotton fibre and seed, and electricity by day and by night are each physically "one thing", but each has different demand schedules. Pigou himself writes of the different demand prices for transporting rich and poor passengers, and valuable and cheap goods. That concedes the point (pp. 381–383).
5. **The monopoly-revenue test.** If rates were discriminating monopoly, railways would earn large monopoly revenue. British railways do not, yet their rates show the same apparent discrimination. Railways are held back by habit, public opinion and regulation. Pigou, "trained in the mathematical school", applies the theory of monopoly price too readily (pp. 383–384).

## Key terms & distinctions
- **Homogeneous (in the relevant sense)**: having the same demand schedule. This is Taussig's test.
- **Classification**: different rates by commodity or passenger class. It is the "salient consequence" of joint cost.
- **Back loading**: return-haul traffic, which Pigou admits is a joint product.
- **Monopoly revenue**: returns above normal profit. This is Taussig's empirical test against Pigou.
- **Don't confuse:** Taussig does *not* say local or distance discrimination comes from joint cost; he concedes those cases. This February note is a different text from his May rejoinder, which Pigou quotes in #18 ("p. 537").
