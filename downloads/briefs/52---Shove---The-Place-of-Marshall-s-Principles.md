## Main claim
The analytical backbone of the *Principles* is a mathematical "completion and generalisation" of Ricardo–Mill. It is not a synthesis with Jevons or the Austrians. To deal with increasing returns, Marshall used three expedients: external economies, market imperfection and the representative firm. The 1930s theory of imperfect competition (MR = MC; entry at AC = AR) is "essentially his" and only a teaching change. But the decline of atomic competition (oligopoly, joint-stock firms), the missing dynamics and Keynes's monetary revolution leave large gaps.

## Argument in steps
1. Marshall translated Mill's Ricardo into differential equations (1867–8). Filling four Ricardian gaps (demand, variable coefficients, supply of labour, supply of capital) leads to Note XXI. His debts were to Cournot and von Thünen, not Jevons (pp. 294–303).
2. It is still a transformation: mutual determination, balance at the margin, substitution as "master-key". The equilibrium of the *firm* comes to the fore because increasing returns must be reconciled with competition. Hence the **three expedients** (pp. 303–305).
3. Biology shapes his view of change ("organic growth"). That is why the long-period supply curves were "cast for a minor role only" (p. 312). Three epochs of English economics: Classical, Ricardian, Marshallian or "reformed-Ricardian" (p. 313).
4. The decay of "atomic" competition dates the theory. The sixth edition's rewording of the "trees of the forest" (joint-stock companies "do not readily die") "knocks away" the main prop of the reconciliation of competition and increasing returns (pp. 319–321).
5. Imperfect competition follows Marshall's lines. The MR curve is "no more than a geometrical version of Marshall's algebra". The real gap is oligopoly and complex controls. Theory has reverted to Ricardo's mechanical method, dynamics are weak, and Keynes demands that money be integrated with value theory. Lasting gains: mutual determination, the margin, short vs long period, elasticity. "You may drive out real cost with a pitchfork" (pp. 321–329).

## Key terms & distinctions
- **Reformed-Ricardian** — Shove's label for the Marshallian epoch.
- **Atomic competition** — competition among many small units. It can be perfect or imperfect.
- **Three expedients** — external economies, market imperfection, and the representative firm (luck and the rise and fall of firms).
- **Don't confuse:** Shove does *not* call Marshall a synthesiser of Ricardo and Jevons. He rejects that view explicitly, against Whittaker and Gray.
