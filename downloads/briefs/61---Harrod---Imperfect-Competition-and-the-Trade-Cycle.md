# 61 · Harrod (1936) — Imperfect Competition and the Trade Cycle
*Review of Economic Statistics, 18(2), pp. 84–88* · Group: E — Imperfect vs monopolistic competition · Tier: 3

**Hook (memorize this line):** Demand gets less elastic in booms and more elastic in slumps, so prices and profits swing more than costs.

**Responds to:** His own #56 (1934) trade-cycle section; psychological trade-cycle theories (context)  
**Answered by:** #63 Chamberlin 1937 (p. 559) quotes Harrod's "leading principle … equate marginal cost to marginal revenue" as a misconception, since MR = MC holds under any market form.

## Main claim
Assume entrepreneurs equate MR and MC within the cycle. The greater amplitude of price swings than reward-cost swings, and of profit swings than price swings, can then be explained without time lags or errors. Rising incomes make buyers careless, so elasticity falls and competition grows more imperfect in booms. In slumps elasticity rises sharply, giving a precipitous fall in prices and profits.

## Argument in steps
1. "The leading principle of the theory of imperfect competition is that entrepreneurs tend to equate marginal cost to marginal revenue." This is taken as a first approximation in the short period (p. 84).
2. *Reward costs* (pay per unit) fluctuate less than prices, so either *work costs* rise in booms or the MR/price ratio falls. Work costs explain only part (p. 85–86).
3. Imperfection is "due to habit, inertia, and lack of knowledge." The rich (and prosperous firms) bother less to find the cheapest market, so in good times demand is less elastic. A slump "recalls him to his senses" (pp. 86–87).
4. Example: average elasticity rising from 4 to 5 in a recession implies prices fall about 6¼% and profits about 25% (p. 88).

## Key terms & distinctions
- **Reward costs vs work costs** — rate of pay per unit of factor vs number of such units per unit of output.
- **Uniform increase** — an output change that leaves relative prices unchanged.
- **Don't confuse:** this explains cycle *amplitudes*, not the *cause* of the cycle.
