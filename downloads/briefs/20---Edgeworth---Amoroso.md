## Main claim
Amoroso is lucid on value and on Cournot's monopoly point. He goes wrong in following Cournot on duopoly. When two monopolists are free to vary price, Amoroso's Cournot equilibrium (Primus 5, Secundus 8) is unstable. Price-cutting is followed by jumps upward whenever one seller serves the "tail" of buyers the other cannot supply. There is therefore no determinate equilibrium. Edgeworth also rejects Amoroso's neutral stance between free trade and protection.

## Argument in steps
1. **Praise (pp. 400–401).** Clear handling of utility, though Edgeworth asks whether a third second-order condition is needed. The "curve of unitary costs" (average cost) is like Pigou's. The Cournot point is shifted by taxes and bounties.
2. **Amoroso's example (p. 402).** Demand is p = 450/(2 + x). The Cournot solution is y = 5, z = 8, price 30. Edgeworth calls this a kartel-like convention.
3. **Instability (pp. 403–405).**
   - Primus's profit is 25 against Secundus's 64, so he cuts price and takes custom; his profit rises to about 79.
   - Cutting continues down to about 21.57.
   - Capacity is limited, so Secundus can charge the unserved "tail" about 47.8 and earn about 80.
   - The jumping goes on "for ever, as long as the monopolists are uncombined".
4. **Verdict (p. 405).** Amoroso is "alone among high authorities in siding with Cournot". Pigou says output under "monopolistic competition" is indeterminate (*Wealth and Welfare*, p. 193). Edgeworth would cut the duopoly section from any English translation.
5. **Complements and trade (pp. 406–407).** Monopolists of complementary goods also "vibrate", and monopolists of rival goods do so a fortiori. A regime in which every industry is monopolised is not comparable with pure competition. On protection, Edgeworth thinks the scales are not evenly balanced.

## Key terms & distinctions
- **Duopoly / competing monopolists**: two sellers of the same article.
- **Cournot point**: the monopoly price-quantity optimum.
- **Unitary cost**: total cost divided by quantity, i.e. average cost.
- **Don't confuse:** Edgeworth does not say Cournot's equations are wrong. He says the quantity-setting convention suits a kartel, not monopolists free to set price.
