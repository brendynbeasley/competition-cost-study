# 36 · G. F. Shove (1928) — Varying Costs and Marginal Net Products

**Hook (memorize this line):** Shove: rent effects must be counted under increasing and diminishing returns alike, so Pigou's asymmetry collapses.

**Responds to:** Pigou, *Economics of Welfare* 2nd ed., Part II ch. x (context, not in your folder); Marshall, *Principles* pp. 472–5 and Appendix H (context)

## Main claim
Pigou (2nd ed.) argued that under diminishing returns the buyers' loss from higher prices is exactly offset by landlords' gain, so social = private net product. Under increasing returns, he said, the buyers' gain is "offset by nothing", so social > private. Shove shows that whether rents offset the price change depends on how expansion affects the *infra-marginal* costs, not on whether returns rise or fall. The tax/bounty doctrine "collapses" in its unqualified form. A valid version must compare marginal cost with "final trade cost", i.e. look at net external economies.

## Argument in steps
1. States Pigou's doctrine (pp. 258–259): diminishing returns, purchasers' burden = landlords' gain, so social = private; increasing returns, purchasers' gain offset by nothing, so social > private.
2. Price = marginal cost = cost of the unit with no differential advantages. Rent = the sum of infra-marginal units' cost advantages. Expansion can change infra-marginal costs too, e.g. haulage economies or port congestion (pp. 259–260).
3. Algebra (output x → x+1): purchasers' burden = (p₂ − p₁)x; landlords' gain = (p₂ − p₁)x − [(c₂ − p₂) − c₁]. They are equal only if (c₂ − p₂) = c₁, i.e. no net economies on the rest of output. That holds "whether returns are diminishing, increasing or constant" (pp. 260–261).
4. With the same assumption, Pigou's claims are true of *both* types or neither. His imported-raw-material case is just an example of that assumption (pp. 262–263).
5. Restatement: final trade cost = c₂ − c₁. The tax/bounty doctrine collapses unqualified, but Marshall's form "remains unshaken". Shift resources toward industries where marginal cost is far above final trade cost (large net external economies). Increasing returns only make this likelier (pp. 265–266).

## Key terms & distinctions
- **Infra-marginal costs** — costs (excluding rent) of the units produced with differential advantages.
- **Final trade cost** — the increase in total cost (excluding rent) when output rises by one unit (c₂ − c₁). It is the social cost of the extra unit.
- **Marginal cost (Shove)** — the cost of the least-favoured unit, which equals price under competition.
- **Don't confuse:** Shove does not say rents are always transfers that cancel. He shows the *same* rent adjustment applies whichever way returns go.
