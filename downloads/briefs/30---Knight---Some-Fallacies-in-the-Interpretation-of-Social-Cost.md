# 30 · F. H. Knight (1924) — Some Fallacies in the Interpretation of Social Cost

**Responds to:** Pigou, *The Economics of Welfare* (1920), p. 194 two-roads example (context, not in your folder); F. D. Graham's 1923 QJE protection articles (context); credits #26 Young 1913 (fn. 8)

## Main claim
Pigou's argument that free enterprise over-invests in increasing-cost industries rests on confusing social cost with entrepreneurs' cost. Once scarce opportunities are privately owned, rent (a toll) stops investment exactly where the marginal product equals that on the free alternative. That is the social optimum. Decreasing cost under competition is also a fallacy: the defects of competition lie outside "the mechanics of exchange".

## Argument in steps
1. Pigou's roads: a broad, poor road that never gets crowded and a narrow, good road that does. Trucks crowd the good road until the cost *per truck* is equal on both. Moving a few trucks off would raise total output, so a tax on the narrow road would be "a clear gain" (pp. 584–586).
2. The logic is fine, but the assumptions reverse the key feature of competition: private ownership. The owner of the narrow road charges a toll equal to its "superiority". To maximise rent, he sets it so the *marginal* truck adds no more than it could on the free road. So "the toll will exactly equal the ideal tax" (pp. 586–588). Same logic as a superior farm versus free land.
3. Charts C and D: think in diminishing *returns* per unit of investment, not increasing cost per unit of output. Pigou was "misled by measuring efficiency in terms of cost of output". Rent is the owner's marginal product and maximising it gives the social optimum (pp. 588–591; fn. 8 credits Young 1913).
4. Cost is the alternative value given up. Increasing cost has "value" causes and "technological" causes. Higher payments to specialised factors (rents) "do not represent social costs at all, but redistributions of product merely" (pp. 592–596).
5. Decreasing cost is a fallacy. Technical economies come from the size of the *unit*, not the output of the industry. External economies are "internal economies in some other" firm within the industry, so lasting economies end in monopoly. Competition is not systematically biased; its real faults lie elsewhere: ignorance, inheritance, monopoly, the state (pp. 596–598, 605–606).

## Key terms & distinctions
- **Social cost vs entrepreneur's cost** — real cost is what transferable resources could produce elsewhere; rent is a transfer.
- **Rent as allocator** — the private owner's charge limits investment to the point where *marginal* product (not product per unit) equals the free alternative.
- **Value vs technological causes of increasing cost** — Knight's pair (p. 594). (Context, not in your folder: later writers call this pecuniary vs technological external effects, after Viner 1931.)
- **Don't confuse:** Knight agrees with Pigou's *logic* for unowned roads; his point is that ownership changes the result. The two-roads example is from Pigou's 1920 book, not from Young 1913.
