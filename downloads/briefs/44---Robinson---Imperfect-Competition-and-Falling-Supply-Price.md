# 44 · Robinson (1932) — Imperfect Competition and Falling Supply Price
*Economic Journal, 42(168), Dec 1932, pp. 544–554* · Group: D2 — The 1930 symposium & decreasing costs · Tier: 1

**Hook (memorize this line):** Robinson: with imperfect competition, equilibrium firms *must* have falling average cost, yet supply price may rise, fall or stay put.

**Responds to:** Sraffa 1926 (context, not in your folder), #38 Symposium 1930, #41 Harrod 1930, #43 Harrod 1931; Kahn's tangency theorem (unpublished, credited p. 547 n.)

## Main claim
Robinson isolates imperfect competition by assuming identical firms, perfectly elastic factor supplies and no economies of large-scale industry. Two conditions must then hold together: MR = MC, and price = AC including normal profit. So the firm's demand curve is *tangent* to its AC curve, and AC must be falling in full equilibrium. But an increase in total demand changes price only through the *elasticity* of the new individual demand curves. So falling supply price is possible but not guaranteed. Informal factors (more firms squeezed in between, chances to specialise) make it "highly probable".

## Argument in steps
1. Assumptions: no time problems, firms always in individual equilibrium, homogeneous factors in elastic supply, no external or "disintegration" economies, identical firms. The market is imperfect only through transport costs or fixed preferences (pp. 544–546).
2. From the individual demand curve comes the **marginal revenue curve** ("the 'increment of aggregate demand curve' of Mr. Harrod", p. 546 n.). The firm is in equilibrium where MR = MC. The industry is in equilibrium where profits are normal, defined as the level that draws in new firms (pp. 546–547).
3. **Double condition:** both hold only where AR is tangent to AC. Kahn's theorem (p. 547). Since AR slopes down, the tangency is on the falling part of AC: average costs "must be falling" (pp. 548–549). She criticises Harrod: the double condition is not an accident, and the motive to merge is monopoly profit (p. 549 n.).
4. **Supply price:** if demand rises, new firms enter until tangency is restored. Unchanged elasticity gives the same price (Fig. 3). Less elastic gives a higher price (Fig. 4). More elastic gives a lower price (Fig. 5) (pp. 550–552).
5. Examples: new suburbs (unchanged), new firms taking the "indifferent fringe" (rise), denser population with firms placed in between (fall, the most frequent). Relaxing assumptions, especially lateral/vertical disintegration held back by imperfection, makes falling supply price "highly probable" (pp. 552–554).

## Key terms & distinctions
- **Marginal revenue curve** — the increment of total receipts from one more unit of the firm's output (her name for Harrod's curve).
- **Double condition of equilibrium** — MR = MC (firm) and price = AC (industry, with normal profit in AC).
- **Tangency (Kahn's) theorem** — the firm's demand curve is tangent to its AC curve in full equilibrium.
- **Don't confuse:** falling *average cost of the firm* (must hold here) vs falling *supply price of the commodity* (may or may not). Also, her supply curve depends on the type of change in demand. Perfect competition is just the simplest assumption (p. 550 n.).
