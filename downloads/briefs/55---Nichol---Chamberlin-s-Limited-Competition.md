# 55 · Nichol (1934) — Professor Chamberlin's Theory of Limited Competition
*Quarterly Journal of Economics, 48(2), pp. 317–337* · Group: E — Imperfect vs monopolistic competition · Tier: 2

**Hook (memorize this line):** Far-sighted duopolists reach the monopoly price only with costless production; change the costs and price moves above or below monopoly.

**Responds to:** Chamberlin, "Duopoly: Value When Sellers Are Few," QJE 1929, and Theory of Monopolistic Competition 1933, ch. III (context); Cournot and Edgeworth on duopoly (context; see group B)  
**Answered by:** No reply in the set (Chamberlin's later responses are context only). Nichol also co-reviewed Robinson in #54.

## Main claim
Chamberlin's oligopoly theorem says that a far-sighted few, acting independently, tend toward the pure monopoly price. It is derived from "one all too simple case" of costless production. It also holds for constant total costs, constant unit costs, or identical increasing costs. But with decreasing variable costs, duopoly price is *higher* than monopoly price. With *unequal* increasing costs it is *lower*.

## Argument in steps
1. Chamberlin's review of Cournot vs Edgeworth (rival's output vs rival's price held constant) does not "get down to bedrock." Cournot's theory makes sense only if buyers name prices; Edgeworth's only if sellers name prices (p. 318).
2. **Decreasing variable cost** (Table I). A monopoly runs one plant and charges 20. Far-sighted duopolists split sales equally and each maximises profit from half the market, at price 24. Duopoly price exceeds monopoly price. A general proof follows in Cournot's symbols (pp. 319–325).
3. **Unequal increasing costs** (Table II). Monopoly price 28, with output split between plants by equal MC. As a duopolist, inefficient M wants 30 and efficient N wants 26. N can cut and M must follow, so equilibrium is 26, below monopoly. A second maximum near 21 is possible (pp. 325–334).
4. Nine contrasts with monopoly: M sells more, N less; total sales higher; production less efficient; no price acceptable to both; buyers gain; total profits lower; N's profits fall; M's profits rise (pp. 335–336).
5. Chamberlin's costless case is "woefully unrepresentative." Limited competition may tend above or below the monopoly price, though the book's terminology and technique are of "permanent importance" (pp. 336–337).

## Key terms & distinctions
- **Limited competition** — competition among a few sellers of the *same* product (duopoly/oligopoly), not product differentiation.
- **Foresight** — each rival anticipates the other's reactions and the indirect effects of his own acts (Chamberlin's contribution).
- **Don't confuse:** this is about Chamberlin's *small-group, homogeneous-product* duopoly, not the large-group tangency.
