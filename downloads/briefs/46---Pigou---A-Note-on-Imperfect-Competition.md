# 46 · Pigou (1933) — A Note on Imperfect Competition
*Economic Journal, 43(169), Mar 1933, pp. 108–112* · Group: D2 — The 1930 symposium & decreasing costs · Tier: 2

**Hook (memorize this line):** Pigou rebuilds Robinson's geometry in algebra: two equations, two unknowns, so her conditions are compatible and determinate.

**Responds to:** #44 Robinson 1932
**Answered by:** none directly in the set. Read alongside #47 Shove 1933 (same issue) and #45 Harrod 1933

## Main claim
Pigou doubted that Robinson's two conditions could always hold together: each firm maximises net monopoly revenue, and no firm wants to enter or leave. He writes them as two equations in price *p* and the number of firms *n*. They are always compatible and the problem is determinate. The algebra confirms and extends her results to firms of unequal size and situation. It also gives a measure of market imperfection.

## Argument in steps
1. Notation: F(p) is total demand at common works price p; f(p) is each firm's output at average cost p; n is the number of firms; ψ(p, n) is the firm's demand when it alone changes price. Conditions: (1) (1/n)F(p) = f(p); (2) (1/n)F′(p) − ∂ψ/∂p = f′(p) (p. 109).
2. The equations still apply when firms are unequal in size (n = how many times the firm's output divides into the total) and unequal in situation (only for firms that are themselves in equilibrium). "Two equations and two unknowns", so compatible and determinate (p. 109).
3. Perfect market: ∂ψ/∂p is infinitely negative, so the firm is at its optimum scale. Any finite imperfection puts it at less than optimum scale, i.e. falling AC. Infinite imperfection gives only nil output (p. 110).
4. Elasticity of individual demand = elasticity of the firm's AC curve. More demand lowers price if it makes individual demand more elastic, and raises it otherwise. If price falls, the number of firms rises less than in proportion to output (pp. 110–111).
5. Individual elasticity = elasticity of aggregate demand − a substitution term. ∂ψ/∂p is "a measure of market imperfection". F differs from the textbook aggregate demand: it sums demand at many points in space (pp. 111–112).

## Key terms & distinctions
- **ψ(p, n)** — the rate at which a firm's customers switch to other firms when it alone raises price; ∂ψ/∂p measures market imperfection.
- **Two elements in individual elasticity** — (1) fewer total purchases by the firm's customers, (2) substitution towards other firms.
- **Don't confuse:** Pigou *confirms* Robinson ("add nothing of significance", p. 111). Shove (#47, same issue) *questions* her. Pigou also notes that if f″(p) < 0 the equilibrium is unstable (p. 110 n.).
