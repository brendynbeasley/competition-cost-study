# 24 · Yntema (1928) — The Influence of Dumping on Monopoly Price
*Journal of Political Economy, 36(6), pp. 686–698* · Group: B Cournot revival & math of monopoly · Tier: 2

**Hook (memorize this line):** Yntema: dumping abroad lowers the home monopoly price if marginal costs fall, and raises it if they rise.

**Responds to:** Viner, *Dumping: A Problem in International Trade* (pp. 102–3), who says dumping leaves the home price unchanged; Graham's review (*AER* 1924), which objected but conceded the "great majority of cases"; Barone; Marshall's Note XXII; Edgeworth's primary and secondary returns (*Papers* I, 67–74; cf. #16)  
**Answered by:** none in your folder (context: Joan Robinson's 1933 theory of price discrimination)

## Main claim
A monopoly that dumps equates the marginal gross revenue in each market to its common marginal cost. Whether the *domestic* price changes depends on how marginal cost slopes:
- with **decreasing** marginal costs, dumping **lowers** the home price;
- with **increasing** marginal costs, dumping **raises** it.

Viner's claim of no effect is therefore wrong. The foreign price is the lower one when foreign demand is the more elastic. Yntema's presumption is that permanent dumping of manufactures under decreasing marginal costs benefits home consumers.

## Argument in steps
1. **The question (p. 686).** Viner says the profit-maximising home price is the same with or without dumping. Graham objected with an unusual demand curve. Yntema, at Viner's suggestion, seeks general conditions.
2. **One market (pp. 687–688).** Maximise xₐyₐ − xₐy_c. Marginal gross revenue equals marginal cost (Fig. 1).
3. **Two markets (pp. 688–690).** Each market's marginal revenue equals the common marginal cost. Add the two marginal revenue curves horizontally (DₐDₐ + D_bD_b) and intersect the sum with marginal cost (Fig. 2). The method works for any number of markets.
4. **Direction of change (pp. 691–693).** Shift the marginal cost curve left by the quantity dumped and see where it cuts home marginal revenue.
   - Decreasing marginal cost: home sales rise and the home price falls.
   - Increasing marginal cost: home sales fall and the home price rises.
   G. T. Jones supplied these constructions.
5. **Extensions (pp. 696–698).**
   - With elasticity of 1 or less there is no interior maximum.
   - The foreign price is lower if and only if foreign demand is more elastic.
   - Short-run dumping with rising marginal cost raises the home price.
   - A "self-restrained" monopoly reaches the same conclusions.
   - Welfare presumption: permanent dumping of manufactures under decreasing marginal cost benefits home consumers.

## Key terms & distinctions
- **Dumping**: selling abroad at a price that discriminates but is "not necessarily below cost". It is price discrimination between markets.
- **Marginal gross revenue**: what we now call marginal revenue.
- **Permanent vs short-run dumping**: whether the fixed plant can be adjusted.
- **Edgeworth's primary sense**: increasing marginal costs mean decreasing returns in the primary sense (fn. 12, p. 696).
- **Don't confuse:** the results turn on *marginal* cost, not average cost. Case IV even shows rising marginal cost alongside falling average cost.
