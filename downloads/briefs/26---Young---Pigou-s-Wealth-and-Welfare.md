# 26 · Allyn A. Young (1913) — Review: Pigou's *Wealth and Welfare*
*Quarterly Journal of Economics, 27(4), Aug 1913, pp. 672–686* · Group: D1 Pigou's welfare economics & the cost controversy to 1928 · Tier: 2

**Hook (memorize this line):** Young: Pigou's "marginal supply price" counts higher land rents as cost, but rents are mere transfers.

**Responds to:** Pigou, *Wealth and Welfare* (1912) (context, not in your folder); notes Edgeworth's approval of Pigou  
**Answered by:** Pigou's reply in *Economics of Welfare* 1920, pp. 934–6 (context); endorsed by Knight #30 (fn. 8), Robertson #31 (§11), Edgeworth #21; Pigou concedes in 1924 (reply appended to #31)

## Main claim
Pigou's "curve of marginal supply prices" (Young: better called the "curve of aggregate expenses") is a fine tool for monopoly, but wrong for judging whether competition maximises the national dividend. Under diminishing returns the extra aggregate expense comes mostly from higher rents for land, which are transfers of purchasing power, not extra resources used up. So Pigou's case for taxing diminishing-return industries and giving bounties to increasing-return industries does not follow.

## Argument in steps
1. Summarises Pigou: the marginal-supply-price curve (SS2) shows how much *aggregate* expenses rise per extra unit; the ordinary supply curve (SS1) lies below it under diminishing returns and above it under increasing returns (pp. 676–679).
2. Pigou's result: competitive output is too large in diminishing-return industries and too small in increasing-return ones, so tax the first and give bounties to the second (p. 680). A perfectly discriminating monopolist, equating demand with SS2, does better (p. 680).
3. Side point (fn., p. 678): increasing returns in a competitive industry are rare unless the representative firm grows; Young "cannot imagine 'external economies'" that would give increasing returns to the industry and diminishing returns to each firm.
4. Core objection (pp. 682–683): Pigou slides between "resources" meaning real services of labour and capital and meaning entrepreneurs' money expenses. The excess of SS2 over SS1 is higher land rent, and "they merely represent transferences of purchasing power."
5. To be consistent, Pigou should measure both cost and product in money, or both in physical units; either way he would get closer to the traditional result that competition maximises the product (pp. 683–684). Young praises the rest of the book.

## Key terms & distinctions
- **Curve of aggregate expenses** — Young's name for Pigou's curve of marginal supply prices: how much an extra unit adds to the industry's *total* expenses, rent included.
- **Transfer vs. using up resources** — higher prices for land are redistributions of purchasing power, not a social cost.
- **Don't confuse:** Young 1913 does **not** use the two-roads example (that is Knight #30, from Pigou's 1920 book). And this Young (1913 review) is not Young 1928 (#37, increasing returns and the division of labour).
