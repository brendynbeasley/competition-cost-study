# 53 · Kaldor (1934) — Mrs. Robinson's "Economics of Imperfect Competition" (review)
*Economica, n.s. 1(3), pp. 335–341* · Group: E — Imperfect vs monopolistic competition · Tier: 3

**Hook (memorize this line):** A brilliant treatise on monopoly misnamed: it leaves out duopoly, marketing costs and the "imagined demand curve."

**Responds to:** Robinson, The Economics of Imperfect Competition (1933) (context); compares Chamberlin 1933 (context)  
**Answered by:** Developed in Kaldor #58 (cites p. 339 on the "industry" and p. 340 on imagined demand). Kahn #59 cites pp. 337–339.

## Main claim
Robinson's book promises a theory of imperfect competition but "inevitably becomes a treatise on monopoly." It omits duopoly (rival interaction) and marketing costs, which are the hallmarks of imperfect markets. It is superb within its range, especially on distribution, and "ultra-Marshallian." Its "industry" concept and its objective demand curve are unsatisfactory. The relevant curve is the producer's *imagined* demand curve.

## Argument in steps
1. Traditional theory assumed cross-elasticities of zero or infinity, but reality is "more or less different" products. Robinson excludes duopoly and marketing costs, so the book is about monopoly (p. 335).
2. Best part: Books VII–IX on distribution. Hired factors get their marginal *value* product, less than the value of their marginal physical product. *Monopsonistic* exploitation (unlike monopolistic) wastes resources technically and can be countered by collective bargaining (pp. 337–339).
3. Her "industry" (a chain of substitutes bounded by gaps) assumes that each producer's demand is equally sensitive to every other price in the group. The village cigarette shop may be closer to the pub's beer than to the town's cigarettes (pp. 339–340).
4. The real demand curve is indeterminate because it depends on rivals' reactions. The *imagined* curve is determinate. The two need meet only at the actual point, so ignorance can persist in equilibrium (pp. 340–341).

## Key terms & distinctions
- **Imagined vs real demand curve** — the subjective image in the entrepreneur's mind vs the objective functional relation.
- **Monopolistic vs monopsonistic exploitation** — the former keeps factor proportions efficient, the latter distorts them.
- **Don't confuse:** praise for the book's technique vs criticism of its title.
