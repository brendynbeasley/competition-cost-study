# 54 · Schumpeter (and Nichol) (1934) — Robinson's Economics of Imperfect Competition (reviews)
*Journal of Political Economy, 42(2), pp. 249–259 (Schumpeter pp. 249–257; Nichol pp. 257–259)* · Group: E — Imperfect vs monopolistic competition · Tier: 3

**Hook (memorize this line):** Schumpeter: marginal revenue unifies price theory, yet the book is "Marshallian to the core"; Nichol: geometry can't handle monopolies.

**Responds to:** Robinson, The Economics of Imperfect Competition (1933) (context)  
**Answered by:** No reply in the set. Compare Kaldor's review #53. Nichol's own #55 on Chamberlin.

## Main claim
**Schumpeter:** perfect competition matters for its *properties*, not its realism or desirability. Marginal revenue gives one formula for price in all cases from perfect competition to monopoly: price = MR × e/(e − 1). Robinson's book is an admirable, perhaps leading, textbook, but Marshallian to the core (partial equilibrium, no Cournot, no indifference curves, no Walras). **Nichol:** the book is mostly about pure monopoly and perfect competition. Its geometric elasticity tools fail in "A World of Monopolies," which needs calculus and simultaneous equations.

## Argument in steps
1. Schumpeter: imperfect competition destroys the presumption of maximum satisfaction, so cases for collective action become "the rule." Beware "the Ricardian error" of rushing to policy (pp. 249–251).
2. MR is the "master key"; "adjusted concavity" helps with discrimination (p. 252).
3. Criticisms: "sensible manner" assumption is *ignotum per ignotius*; immediate-gain maximisation; ethics. Agenda: time, money, the cycle, and dropping the supply-demand apparatus (pp. 253–257).
4. Nichol: elasticity concept assumes other prices or supplies constant, which fails in a world of monopolies; recommends Fisher's *Calculus for Economists* (pp. 257–259).

## Key terms & distinctions
- **Single price formula** — price = MR × e/(e − 1).
- **Adjusted concavity** — Robinson's second-derivative measure, useful for price discrimination.
- **Don't confuse:** this item contains *two* reviews (Schumpeter's, then Nichol's).
