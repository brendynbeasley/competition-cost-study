# 21 · F. Y. Edgeworth (1925) — The Revised Doctrine of Marginal Social Product
*Economic Journal, 35(137), Mar 1925, pp. 30–39* · Group: D1 Pigou's welfare economics & the cost controversy to 1928 · Tier: 2

**Hook (memorize this line):** Old Edgeworth defends Pigou's anti-laissez-faire conclusion but admits treating rents as cost was an error.

**Responds to:** Pigou, *Economics of Welfare* 2nd ed. 1924 (context, not in your folder), compared with *Wealth and Welfare* 1912 and the 1920 1st ed.; #26 Young 1913; #31 Robertson 1924  
**Answered by:** No direct reply in folder; Pigou's restatements #33 (1927) and #35 (1928) carry the revised doctrine forward

## Main claim
Reviewing the 1924 edition, Edgeworth rebuilds the theory with his own diagrams. The curve of "marginal supply prices" is drawn so the area under it equals total cost. Output is best where demand meets that curve. This gives a real case against laissez-faire under increasing returns. But when landlords are members of the community, counting rent as a cost is "an error" (Young; Clark). Correcting it barely harms the main result: the blow the theory deals to the abstract doctrine of laissez-faire (p. 39).

## Argument in steps
1. Complains that Pigou keeps rearranging his treatise (the Sibyl's scattered leaves). Collates the three versions around "marginal supply price" and Marshall's external economies (p. 30).
2. Formal construction: each firm's supply depends on price and on aggregate output; fixing aggregate output gives a short-period curve, letting it vary gives the long-period curve. Draws a marginal-supply-price curve STQ whose area equals total cost (pp. 31–34).
3. Increasing returns are real (against Young's doubt), citing Robertson's own list of external economies. External *diseconomies* exist too, e.g. imported raw materials rising in price, or a fanciful Irish Free State with absentee landlords (pp. 33–35).
4. Welfare use: the net contribution to income (total utility minus aggregate cost) is greatest where demand meets the marginal-supply-price curve. Credits Young's name "curve of aggregate expenses" and welcomes dropping "marginal supply price". But Young's paraphrase wrongly makes it sound like laissez-faire (pp. 35–37).
5. Weighs Robertson's objections (the future, inventions, *pennis non homini datis*): the theory is "curious and may be useful". Finally concedes that if landlords are citizens, rents are not cost, and admits "we ourselves have fallen" into the same error (pp. 38–39).

## Key terms & distinctions
- **Marginal social net product** — Pigou's 1924 term replacing plain "marginal net product". Edgeworth finds the verbal statement ambiguous without diagrams.
- **Curve of aggregate expenses / marginal supply prices** — the area under it equals the industry's total cost; set against demand to find the best output.
- **Don't confuse:** Edgeworth concedes the *rent* error but **not** the increasing-returns case for bounties. He is defending Pigou's conclusion, not abandoning it.
